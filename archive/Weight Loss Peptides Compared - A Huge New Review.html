Weight Loss Peptides Compared: A Huge New Review

Which weight loss peptides work best, and what do they cost you in side effects? A new review in the BMJ, one of the world's top medical journals, tried to answer both. It pooled 262 trials and nearly 100,000 adults. The short version: the strongest options also come with the biggest trade-offs.

What Are Weight Loss Peptides?

A peptide is a short chain of amino acids, the building blocks of protein. Your body makes many of them. Some act as hormones that tell your brain you are full.

Weight loss peptides copy those signals. Semaglutide, tirzepatide and liraglutide are the best known. They mimic a gut hormone called GLP-1, which slows digestion and turns down appetite. Tirzepatide copies a second hormone, GIP, as well. CagriSema pairs semaglutide with cagrilintide, which imitates a third hormone, amylin.

Not every drug in the review is a peptide. Phentermine-topiramate and naltrexone-bupropion are older pills. Orforglipron is a newer pill that is not a peptide. The reviewers compared them all, which is what makes the results useful. Note that these are prescription medicines, not the "research peptides" sold online.

What the Weight Loss Peptides Review Found

A team led by Kailei Nong and Sheyu Li searched medical databases up to November 2025. They kept randomized trials lasting at least 12 weeks. That left 262 trials, 99,791 participants and 19 drugs. They then ran a network meta-analysis, a method that compares drugs never tested head to head by linking them through shared comparisons, such as placebo.

After one year, compared with lifestyle changes alone, tirzepatide led to about 14.9% more body weight lost. CagriSema was nearly identical at 14.8%. Oral semaglutide followed at 10.9%, then orforglipron at 9.9%, injected semaglutide at 9.8% and phentermine-topiramate at 8.1%. For someone who weighs 200 pounds, 15% is about 30 pounds.

The harms tracked the benefits. Stomach problems such as nausea were most common with naltrexone-bupropion, oral semaglutide, orforglipron and tirzepatide. Fatigue rose most with naltrexone-bupropion, orforglipron and CagriSema. Tirzepatide also cut the most muscle: fat mass fell 25.7% and lean mass fell 8.3%.

Injected semaglutide was the only drug linked to fewer deaths and fewer heart attacks, though that evidence mostly came from people already at high heart risk. No drug clearly lifted quality of life.

There are limits. Newer drugs such as retatrutide, mazdutide and ecnoglutide may do as well or better, but the evidence for them is still thin. The authors rate their certainty as low to very low. Until larger, longer trials arrive, treat those numbers as early hints rather than settled facts.

Why It Matters for You

If you follow the headlines, you might assume the newest drug always wins. This review says it is more complicated. Two options sit at the top among weight loss peptides and pills, but the gap to the rest is smaller than you might expect. Injected semaglutide, at roughly 10%, still delivers a lot.

The heart findings may matter more than the scale for some people. If you live with heart disease, injected semaglutide has the strongest evidence behind it. If weight is your only goal, the comparison looks different.

The muscle numbers deserve attention too. Losing muscle can leave you weaker, so it is worth asking about strength training and protein. I found the quality-of-life result the most striking. People lost real weight, yet the review found no drug that lifted quality of life by a clearly meaningful amount. Keep that in mind when you weigh the trade-offs.

One more thing is easy to miss. The results are averages. Averages hide a lot of variation between people. Your own response could be higher or lower, which is one more reason to follow up with your doctor rather than chase a single number.

What to Know Before Trying Weight Loss Peptides

These are prescription medicines. The review covers adults with overweight or obesity who took part in trials, and real-world results can differ.

Side effects are common. Stomach upset is the usual complaint, and many people stop treatment because of it. For several drugs, the chance of quitting over side effects was 1.9 to 4.2 times higher than the comparison group.

Talk to a doctor about your heart, kidneys, any history of pancreatitis or thyroid problems, and pregnancy plans. Ask what happens when you stop, and how to protect your muscle while you lose fat.

Be wary of products sold online as research peptides. They are not the approved drugs tested in these trials, and nobody checks their contents the way medicines are checked. The authors recommend shared decision making, meaning you and your doctor weigh benefits and harms together.

The Bottom Line

Weight loss peptides can deliver large results. Tirzepatide and CagriSema lead at about 15% after a year. Bigger results usually bring more side effects, more dropouts and some muscle loss. Only injected semaglutide showed a clear benefit for survival and heart attacks. The right choice depends on your health, your goals and what you can tolerate. Take this review to your doctor and decide together, and ask them to check in with you along the way.
