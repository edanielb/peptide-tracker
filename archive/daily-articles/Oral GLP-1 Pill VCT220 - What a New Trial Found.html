Oral GLP-1 Pill VCT220: What a New Trial Found

Would you take a daily pill instead of a weekly shot to lose weight? That is the question behind a new oral GLP-1 medicine called VCT220. In a 16-week trial, people on the highest doses lost close to 10% of their body weight.

What Is an Oral GLP-1 Medicine?

GLP-1 is a hormone your gut releases after you eat. It tells your brain you are full, slows digestion and helps your body handle blood sugar. Hormones like this are built from peptides, which are short chains of amino acids, the building blocks of protein.

Popular weight-loss drugs copy GLP-1, and most are injected. A peptide swallowed as a pill gets digested like any other protein, so it rarely survives the stomach.

VCT220 takes a different route. It is a nonpeptide, meaning a small chemical that is not an amino acid chain. It still fits the GLP-1 receptor, the docking spot on your cells that the hormone normally uses. Think of a key cut from a different metal that opens the same lock. Because it is not a protein, it can work as a pill taken once a day.

What the Oral GLP-1 Trial Found

The study ran at 13 sites in China and included 250 adults. All had obesity, or overweight plus at least one related health condition. Researchers assigned them at random, three to one, to VCT220 (189 people) or a placebo (61 people). Nobody knew who got which. Everyone also received lifestyle counseling. After 16 weeks, 237 people were still in the trial.

The placebo group lost 1.61% of body weight on average. People taking 80 mg of VCT220 lost 5.75%, and 120 mg brought 7.42%. At 160 mg, the loss was about 9.4% to 9.7%, depending on how fast the dose was raised. Every dose beat placebo by a wide margin.

The share of people who lost at least 5% of their weight ranged from 55.4% on the lowest dose to 90.3% on 160 mg with slow dose increases. On placebo, it was 13.1%. Up to 45.2% on the top dose lost 10% or more, versus 1.6% on placebo.

Blood pressure fell by 5.2 to 9.7 mmHg with VCT220 and 3.1 with placebo. Blood sugar and insulin markers also improved. The authors treat those as early signals only. The paper appeared in August 2026 in Signal Transduction and Targeted Therapy. The drug maker paid for it, and two authors work for the company.

Why This Oral GLP-1 Result Matters for You

A pill could appeal to anyone who dislikes needles. It might also be easier to make in large amounts, which could matter for supply. Nothing in this study says what VCT220 would cost or whether it will ever reach your pharmacy.

For many people, the biggest hurdle with an injection is the injection itself. An oral GLP-1 option removes that step. It could also fit more easily into a morning routine, since this pill was taken once a day.

Some perspective helps. Losing nearly 10% in 16 weeks is a solid result for a pill. Injectable drugs have posted bigger numbers, but over longer trials, so you can't compare them directly.

I find the blood pressure result the most interesting part. Weight loss is the headline, but a drop of up to 9.7 mmHg in a few months hints at wider benefits. Still, it was an exploratory measure, so treat it as a clue.

Who took part matters too. The average age was 32, most were Han Chinese, and the trial was short. Your results could differ. It also did not test VCT220 against any other treatment. So read this as an encouraging first look, not a verdict.

What to Know Before Trying an Oral GLP-1 Pill

You can't get VCT220 today. It is still in phase II testing, an early stage that checks whether a drug works and how safe it looks. Be wary of any website selling pills labeled with this name.

Side effects were mostly stomach-related. Digestive problems hit 58.4% of people on the drug versus 27.9% on placebo. They peaked while doses were rising, and a faster ramp-up caused more trouble: 80.6% versus 64.5% at 160 mg. Most cases were mild to moderate, and few people quit. Mild low blood sugar occurred in 2% of participants. The authors report no deaths and no serious problems tied to the drug.

Sixteen weeks says little about long-term safety. The authors themselves say trials of at least a year are needed.

Think of this trial as a preview. An oral GLP-1 pill that works as well as early data suggest would be a real shift, but phase II results often shrink once larger groups are tested over a longer time.

If weight is a concern for you, ask your doctor which approved options fit your health history and medications. Ask what happens if you stop treatment, too. This trial can't answer that.

The Bottom Line on the Oral GLP-1 Pill

This oral GLP-1 pill is an early-stage drug that mimics a gut hormone and led to meaningful weight loss in a 16-week trial. The stomach side effects were common but mostly manageable. We still need longer, larger studies before anyone knows if it holds up. For now, it is one to watch, not one to ask for.
